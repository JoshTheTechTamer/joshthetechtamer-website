Right now, before anything else

Something feels off? Start here.

These steps are safe to do on your own while you wait to hear back from me.

Don’t call, click, or pay.

If a pop-up, text, or caller says you owe money, your computer is infected, or your account is locked, stop there. Don’t call the number, don’t let anyone connect to your computer, and never pay with gift cards.

Close it or shut it down.

Close the browser. If the warning won’t go away, hold the power button to turn the computer off. If you already gave someone access, keep it off the internet until we can check it.

Call your bank if money was involved.

Shared card or bank details, or sent a payment? Call your bank using the number on the back of your card. Then text or call me at 563-261-0200.

The usual suspects. Fake “virus” pop-ups with a phone number to call. Calls or texts pretending to be Microsoft, Amazon, your bank or a delivery company. A Facebook or email account that suddenly starts messaging your friends. When one account gets taken over, others that share the same password often follow. If anyone asks for gift cards, remote access to your computer, or a code sent to your phone, stop and check first. Untangling it goes one account at a time, then 2-step verification makes it much harder to happen again.

Good questions

Scam help: the quick answers.

A pop-up says my computer is infected and gives a phone number. Should I call?

No. Real companies like Microsoft and Apple don’t put phone numbers in warning pop-ups. Don’t call, don’t let anyone connect to your computer, and don’t pay. Close the browser, or turn the computer off if it won’t close, and text me.

I already let someone onto my computer. What now?

Don’t panic. Turn the computer off or disconnect it from the internet. If you shared banking details or paid anything, call your bank using the number on the back of your card. Then call me, and we’ll check the computer and change the passwords that matter, from a safe device.

Can you get my hacked account back?

I’ll work through every recovery option the company offers with you, step by step. I can’t promise every account can be recovered, because that decision is up to the company. If there’s a way back in, we’ll find it together.

What does scam help cost?

The same as any tech help: $50 an hour, and the first conversation is free. On-site visits have a one-hour minimum. I’ll never ask you for gift cards.

How do I keep this from happening again?

Turn on 2-step verification for your email and important accounts, use a different password for each one, and pause before you click. My Family Scam Safety Kit has a simple Stop. Check. Call. plan you can keep by the phone.

Report it, too. You can report scams to the Federal Trade Commission at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. It helps them warn others. Need other tech help? See tech help in Muscatine or the areas I serve.
